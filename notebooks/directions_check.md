## 2. Drought Assistance Targeting: a 2000–2015 ranking checked on 2016–2025

Rank counties by how often their growing season had severe drought in 2000–2015 (mean weeks in D2+); check whether the ranking predicts 2016–2025 drought weeks and drought-year corn losses. Chance = Spearman ρ of 0.

- 2000–2015 drought weeks vs 2016–2025 drought weeks: Spearman ρ = **0.63** (p = 6.2e-26, 224 counties). The ranking holds.
- 2000–2015 drought weeks vs 2016–2025 corn loss in drought seasons: ρ = **-0.16** (p = 0.02, 194 counties): drought-prone counties are not the ones that lose most when drought comes (irrigation and soil matter more), so help should be triggered by current conditions, not history.
- The county report card is the county profile in the app and the "Drought report card" report template; the trigger is the 4- and 8-week severe-drought chance (BENCHMARKS.md §3).

## 3. Heat-Stress Advisory: a heat rule chosen on 2000–2018, checked on 2019–2025

Warn when the rule fires; a warning is right if the county's corn or soybean yield ended 10% or more below normal (Iowa and Nebraska). The baseline rule is the master's `extreme_heat_days` at the threshold that works best on 2000–2018; the alternative adds drought (weeks in D2+), since heat without dry soil rarely hurts.

| Rule                                             |   k chosen on 2000–2018 | Hit rate 2000–2018   | False alarms 2000–2018   | Hit rate 2019–2025   | False alarms 2019–2025   | Hits minus false alarms 2019–2025   |
|:-------------------------------------------------|------------------------:|:---------------------|:-------------------------|:---------------------|:-------------------------|:------------------------------------|
| Days at 35 °C or hotter ≥ k                      |                       7 | 61%                  | 27%                      | 62%                  | 17%                      | +44%                                |
| Days at 35 °C or hotter ≥ k AND ≥ 2 weeks in D2+ |                       2 | 69%                  | 21%                      | 78%                  | 33%                      | +45%                                |

Adding drought to the heat rule catches more losses on the unseen years at the cost of more false alarms; net, the two rules tie. Heat days alone are not a sufficient warning (38% of losses missed), and heat plus drought is a wider net. The app warns on both, day by day.
307 losses in 1983 county-seasons, 2019–2025. The app's in-season yield model uses heat, hot nights and drought together with monotonic constraints (BENCHMARKS.md §2).

## 4. Soil and Crop Resilience: does soil water storage cut drought losses?

Corn in Iowa and Nebraska, seasons with the worst drought level at D2 or worse. Compare the mean yield anomaly of counties in the top third of soil water storage (AWS, top 100 cm) with the bottom third; range from 2,000 bootstrap resamples of county-seasons.

- Drought seasons: 1640 county-seasons (2000–2025); AWS terciles at 171 and 184 mm.
- Mean yield anomaly, high-storage counties: **-6.8%**; low-storage: **-4.0%**.
- Soil effect (high minus low): **-2.8 points**, 90% range -4.1 to -1.5. Counties with more soil water storage did **not** lose less; they lost slightly more. Storage does not separate drought losses at county level once irrigation (Nebraska) and the 2012 drought (Iowa) are in the mix, so soil programs cannot be targeted by this field alone.
